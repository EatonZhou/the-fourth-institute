Acquisition with new tissue in an externally supplied, density-regulated material host.

(A) Actual compact-body density fields at life 0 and after 256 acquisition steps under the fast controller, for meanings g0 (safe region on the left) and g1 (safe region on the right). The four views are GUI crops of saved simulation frames; green and red contours mark safe and harmful regions. Final-64-step mean absolute safe contact was 75.14 and 68.96, respectively. Tissue born after life 0 constituted 83.22% and 83.70% of the body at step 256. These are retained endpoint material shares, not spatial cohort maps or survival fractions of all births. Births coexist with net growth and do not establish one-for-one replacement of old material.

(B) Complete acquisition/reversal comparison for two meanings, compact/long bodies, and fast/fixed controllers (eight conditions). Bars show mean safe-contact share during the final 64 steps of acquisition (0–256) and meaning reversal (256–512). C/L denote compact/long; 0/1 denote meaning; F/X denote fast/fixed. Both compact fast conditions passed the acquisition gate; neither compact fixed condition nor either long fast condition passed. None of the eight conditions passed the complete reversal gate. Passing additionally required the prespecified absolute-contact, survival, morphology, and newborn-material criteria; bar height alone does not define success. Long-body expression tails after step 512 are missing and were not imputed or scored as failed retention.

(C) Each actual acquired compact parent at step 256 was forked into 128-step stop-writing futures with newborn-state copying, same-tariff zeroing, or a norm-preserving swap. Physical feedback and action consequences remained active. Safe-contact bars are final-64-step means; orange bars are the final body fraction retained from births since step 256. The g0 physical/action trajectories coincide (safe-contact share 0.88375); g1 shares are 0.83241, 0.83326, and 0.82582. Newborn endpoint fractions span 0.58798–0.60466. The same tariff does not imply identical realized cumulative fees when subsequent births differ. All six futures retained safe contact; these observations do not support necessity of correct newborn copying in this finite window. The three futures from each parent are related interventions, not independent replicates.

This host uses density-regulated material birth/death, external supply, and central action; it is not an original Lenia-kernel growth demonstration.
