\section{Supplementary evidence index}\label{supp:index}
The following records preserve the completed experiments and their original interpretation boundaries. Their batch-specific words ``gate'', ``useful renewal'' and ``maintenance'' retain the operational definitions below; they are not a universal definition of adaptation. Reported failures are not reclassified. Positive fixed-rule physiology is kept separate from learned behaviour. Detailed results and original comparisons remain together so that compression of the main text does not remove counterexamples or costs.

\begin{description}
\item[Evidence and behavioural revision.] Supplementary Section~\ref{supp:detailed-results}, its first two subsections, and Table~\ref{tab:contact} give all fixed-contact and reduced-host comparisons; Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:local-evidence} and Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:carried-evidence} retain their distinct implementations. Supplementary Section~\ref{supp:auxiliary} gives auxiliary cue and predictive-readout tests.
\item[Acquisition, expenditure and physiology.] Table~\ref{tab:continuous} and the detailed results preserve fixed residence, recent feedback, extinction and underpayment. Supplementary Section~\ref{supp:legacy} retains the earlier body, injury, supply and protection comparisons, including finite fixed physiological positive cases and failures.
\item[Formation and finite replacement.] The detailed results' new-body subsection retains all formation and turnover candidates, second-parent coverage and selection timing, rule swaps, generation training, request coordination, birth-site pulses, deletion layouts, preset-ring positives and history/resource controls. Their full protocols are in Supplementary Section~\ref{supp:detailed-methods}; no extra physical comparisons are implied.
\item[Generation, preservation and postbirth support.] Tables~\ref{tab:renewal-choice-outcomes}--\ref{tab:birth-clock-accounting} retain every factorial arm, actual charges, cohort crossover, birth-clock weights and age-supported counterexamples. These include the ring failures and the two useful $G$-history separated-body cases under the original local screen.
\item[Alternative policies and source heterogeneity.] Tables~\ref{tab:conversion-birth-branch}, \ref{tab:response-pooling}, \ref{tab:history-retention-forecast}, \ref{tab:paired-material-fate} and \ref{tab:material-carried-choice} distinguish interventions, prediction and passive accounting. Their original joint-screen failures, conditional positives, missing-field coverage and computation costs remain explicit.
\item[Physical effort and opportunity.] Tables~\ref{tab:effort-comparisons} and \ref{tab:effort-costs} retain every fixed-effort/resource-width branch and its paired trade-offs. No learned width response is inferred from these continuations.
\item[Reproduction and source identity.] Supplementary Section~\ref{supp:source-register} and \path{work/method_renewal/source_register.json} bind the frozen source packages. Existing original reports, CSVs, actual states, costs, failures and figures remain available at their registered paths. The continuing-body cue assay, restricted scalar-action sequence, selected-action history controls and conditional maintenance comparisons are described in the main text; their source packages and local summaries are indexed at the end of the source register.
\end{description}
