\subsection*{Levels of state change and experimental units}
We distinguish mutable lifetime state, lifetime-fixed rule parameters and outer selection. A rule can implement learning without changing its parameters: internal evidence, eligibility and action preferences can change after contact. Outer selection changes the rule between candidates. Inherited rule or initial-state variants are therefore not counted as lifetime experience. Resource transport and paid copying are physical capabilities; their presence alone is not a learning intervention. The experiments use several explicitly distinct models. We do not identify token utility with physical energy, direct density translation with local actuation, or retained protection with associative evidence.

For paired bodily continuations, branches share the entire specified starting body and world, not only density or nominal shape. Reused prefixes are counted once as source computation. Complete paths include extinct tails. Shared histories and factorial combinations are dependent comparisons, not independent replications. Model-specific source tables retain clocks, doses, domains, positive and negative intervals, cumulative payments and zero states. Different initial windows (formation-inclusive or post-formation) are reported separately. A common formation prefix cancels in paired differences but still contributes to absolute complete-path outcomes.

\subsection*{Delayed contact task and lifetime-fixed plasticity}
The controller task has sixty-four fixed contact units on a periodic $32\times32$ lattice. A selected active unit receives a binary cue $c\in\{-1,+1\}$ and chooses a port $a\in\{-1,+1\}$. The hidden mapping belongs to the environment. The rule receives only its current cue and local fields before choosing, and the chosen port's contact bit $h\in\{0,1\}$ after a delay. Resource is replenished across trials. Local eligibility $e=ca$ is retained for the outward/return trip. Units cannot issue another contact while busy. Activation probability is 0.35 and contact noise is 0.15. Evaluation uses ninety-six trials, with a two-step delay except in the five-step long-delay condition. These are decision opportunities in a controller model, not body updates.

The stable schedule has randomly assigned binary contingencies shared within two-by-two blocks of contact units. The checkerboard schedule instead assigns opposite contingencies to adjacent units. Every nonstable schedule reverses its mapping halfway through evaluation, including the checkerboard and longer-delay schedules. The unseen-sequence condition adds a further reversal at three quarters of the run and changes the cue sequence. Thus the current evaluation does not independently factorialize every temporal and spatial change. Actual resource placement uses the mapping at arrival; that hidden mapping is not supplied to the rule before contact.

The separated rule stores direct fields $(n_i,z_i)$ and message fields $(b_i,v_i)$. Direct evidence and the communicated estimate are $m_i=n_i/(z_i+\epsilon)$ and $\bar m_i=b_i/(v_i+\epsilon)$. The message weight, action value and probability are
\begin{align}
w_i&=\beta\exp[-p z_i|m_i-\bar m_i|],\\
\widetilde m_i&=\frac{n_i+w_i b_i}{z_i+w_i v_i+\epsilon},\qquad
\Pr(a_i=+1\mid c_i)=\operatorname{sigmoid}(2g c_i\widetilde m_i).
\end{align}
After a delayed chosen-port contact, $y_i=e_i(2h_i-1)$ updates only the direct fields:
\begin{align}
\alpha_i&=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(a_0+a_1|y_i-m_i|/2,0,1\right)\exp(-d/\tau),\\
n_i'&=(1-\alpha_i)n_i+\alpha_i y_i,\qquad
z_i'=(1-\alpha_i)z_i+\alpha_i.
\end{align}
Only returning contacts write these fields. All four channels decay by $1-f$. At contact sites with positive direct confidence, the decayed direct pair replaces the outgoing message pair; this is copying an estimate, not adding an independent observation. Four nearest-neighbour message-exchange microsteps follow, with symmetric conductance
\begin{align}
D_{ij}&=D\exp[-b\min(v_i,v_j)|\bar m_i-\bar m_j|],\\
J_{ij}^{(s)}&=D_{ij}(s_j-s_i),\qquad s\in\{b,v\}.
\end{align}
Pairwise flux contributions are antisymmetric. Direct fields do not diffuse. No exchange retains decay and contact writes but disables both message injection and exchange and sets $w_i=0$. No arbitration keeps message dynamics but sets $w_i=\beta$; it does not remove the message-field conductance gate. Messages can contain correlated or recirculated information, including a site's own prior output.

The selected parameters are $a_0=0.333900$, $a_1=0.075008$, $f=0.021365$, $D=0.180912$, $b=0.631847$, $g=11.636492$, $\tau=4.699519$, $\epsilon=0.011563$, $\beta=1.108643$ and $p=8.227328$; exact values are in the frozen rule file. An earlier mixed-state rule exchanged its direct evidence and confidence fields. That first development round used sixteen generations of twelve eight-parameter candidates. After its conflict failure, the separated rule was initialized from that development and selected using twenty generations of sixteen ten-parameter candidates on the same four training seeds, stable and reversal conditions, and seventy-two trials. Parameters remain fixed within a lifetime and throughout evaluation; field updates remain active. This outer/inner distinction follows established learning-to-learn formulations\cite{duan,wang}.

Repeated evaluation on the original twelve worlds is labelled development reuse. The second round also evaluates twelve fresh seeds (202610181101--202610181112) across five schedules and ten methods. Its structure was chosen after the first-round failure, so it is not a fully preregistered study. The two completed rounds consumed 2,084 and 3,764 prototype rollouts, respectively, without bodily, independent energy or prepare steps. New confirmation worlds do not supply independent outer-trained rules.

\subsection*{A physiological bound in the reduced ecology}
The reduced model's frozen coefficients impose a constraint independent of the controller. Let $U_t$ denote actual uptake and suppose basal maintenance is fully paid, with no basal-starvation loss. Turnover and uptake then give
\begin{align}
M_{t+1}-M_t &= -\tau M_t+gU_t,\\
E_{t+1}-E_t &\leq \eta U_t-bM_t,
\end{align}
where $\tau=0.0002$, $g=0.12$, $\eta=0.7$ and $b=0.0022$. Movement, memory, energy lost with tissue and capacity spill can only tighten the second inequality. Eliminating uptake yields
\begin{equation}
\Delta\!\left(E-\frac{\eta}{g}M\right)
\leq -\left(b-\frac{\eta\tau}{g}\right)M
= -0.001033\overline{3}\,M.
\end{equation}
Thus bounded mass with a positive long-run mean and nonnegative energy cannot persist indefinitely while every basal bill is paid. Equivalently, periodic mass balance would require basal expenditure of $bg/\tau=1.32$ per unit uptake, exceeding the uptake energy $\eta=0.7$ before other payments. These statements follow from the frozen balance equations; they do not exclude finite persistence, shrinking bodies or starvation--regrowth cycles. Nor do they identify the cause of every observed finite failure. Changing allocation or coefficients to remove this necessary obstruction would not by itself prove sustainable adaptation. The regulated host below caps new mass and rejects precursors, changing the first balance equation; the bound is not applied to that host.
