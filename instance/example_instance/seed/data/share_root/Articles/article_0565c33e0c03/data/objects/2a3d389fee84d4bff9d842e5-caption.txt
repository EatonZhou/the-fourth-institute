Acquired response gain is usable without relearning the reversal. (A) A standard local score learner acquires a scalar gain κ, initially zero, multiplying the direction of the previous actually paid local gradient. Sensory direction and the local actuator are preset. Acquisition and unannounced reversal each last 320 steps; the final 128 steps add no new paid observations or parameter writes. (B) All final-64 field scores and original full-gate outcomes are shown for four methods in both conditions. Learned gain and fixed κ = 1 each pass all phases in both conditions; source bias and zero gain do not. (C) Two actual acquired parents at step 320 are continued with all κ writes frozen. Retaining or mass-mean homogenizing κ passes both later stages; erasure fails. The actual mean command changes direction after the first new paid gradient despite frozen κ. Final fields retain original 32 × 32 periodic coordinates and a common density scale; green/red indicate the positive/negative evaluation field. Rev and No fb denote reversal and no-new-feedback scores, not snapshot values. These are paired forks of two parents, not six independent replicates. The host has no tissue birth, M = 24, preset sensing and execution, and actual paid local outcomes and physical movement costs; computation and communication are unpriced. The results establish a usable acquired gain, not an advantage over the fixed controller or a requirement to relearn the reversal.
